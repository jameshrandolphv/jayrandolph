# Changelog

## 1.10.0
- Chaos Park 1.1: the difficulty climbs much more slowly after 10 laps (at 0.4 of the rate, so laps 10 to 35 play like 10 to 20 used to), and a visitor more can come in every 5 levels instead of 4. From 150 laps one more can come in every 10 laps, up to 26
- Chaos Park: patches arrive at 50 and 100 laps (were 25, 50 and 100), the second carriage at 110 laps (was 10), and run groups from 12 laps, every 20 to 50 seconds until 50 laps
- Chaos Park: carriages now take tokens, except a duplicate or extra laps, and what they take lasts them two laps
- Chaos Park: a new autopilot token (a steering wheel). Whoever takes it glows blue and changes lane, or slows down behind someone slower, to stay out of collisions for the rest of their laps
- Chaos Park: streaks. Every 10 laps in a row without losing a life makes each lap worth a point more (x2, x3...); losing a life starts it again
- Chaos Park: dusk falls from lap 120, and cars switch on their headlights and carriages their lanterns
- Chaos Park: bikers turn up more often (about one visitor in four, was one in six), so the first ones come sooner
- Chaos Park: the streak shows as "10 STREAK" in the middle of the screen for a moment, then a gold glow round the edges of the screen while it lasts
- Chaos Park: bikers and cars are a little slower (about 6%)
- Chaos Park: now and then a slow visitor comes round with a walking frame or in a wheelchair, and some cars are police cars
- Chaos Park: back on the title, the park keeps the season of the one just played, and a crowd of visitors on autopilot goes round behind the title and how-to-play cards
- Chaos Park: the how-to-play rules sit centred between the title and GOT IT!, with shorter wording
- Chaos Park: a sound button at the bottom left of the title switches sound on and off (the same switch as the menu bar's)

## 1.9.3
- Chaos Park: six new tokens, ten in all, and a token's effect now lasts for the rest of the visitor's laps instead of a few seconds. A rare rainbow star flashes its taker through the rainbow and knocks anyone it touches off the track with no life lost, scoring the laps they had left (a carriage scores nothing, and another comes along later). Fire gives super speed (1.5x) and a turtle halves it; strength adds two laps; a coin adds a point; a skull is a wreck that costs a life (unless they have a star); and a spiral slows the whole game to half speed for 10 seconds before easing back. Carriages and run groups still can't take tokens
- Chaos Park: run groups come 10 to 45 seconds after you reach 10 laps, and again 10 to 45 seconds after the last one has gone, instead of every 45 to 65 seconds
- Chaos Park: the ball fields' lines are fainter
- Chaos Park: a heart you gain grows in instead of popping big, so every heart by the lap counter is the same size
- Chaos Park: the how-to-play card is a little taller with more room round and between its rules, drops the tokens row, and sums up lives with one heart: "Too many collisions and the game is over!"

## 1.9.2
- Chaos Park: the hearts by the lap counter show only the lives you have left; a lost one simply goes instead of turning into an empty outline
- Chaos Park: the pause button sits a little further in from the corner, clear of the park's rounded edge

## 1.9.1
- Chaos Park: lap numbers sit in the middle of their circles (they were a little low and to the right)
- Chaos Park: tokens stay on the road for 18 seconds instead of 12, and a heart on the road is drawn as a plain heart like the ones by the lap counter
- Chaos Park: empty hearts keep the same outline as full ones, so a heart no longer looks smaller once it is lost
- Chaos Park: the pause button is gray, and the trophy on the title card is level with "Your best"

## 1.9.0

- Chaos Park: two more tokens on the road. A duplicate adds a copy of the visitor right behind it (it can't cause a collision as it appears), and invisibility makes the visitor see-through for a few seconds so it passes through everyone else
- Chaos Park: tokens start appearing once you have 5 laps, one at a time, and fade away if nobody takes them. The how-to-play card explains them

## 1.8.1
- Bat Out!: much easier to hit. A swipe now meets the ball where you see it; before, it was checked against where the pitch would end up, so swiping through the ball a moment early went over it. Your bat is also wider and more forgiving of timing than the computer's
- Bat Out!: a gold ring on the plate shows roughly where each pitch is heading, and a soft band round your swipe shows how wide your bat is
- Bat Out!: full screen now fills the whole screen instead of leaving a gap on the right
- The dock shows the desktop and the three newest apps, so it fits on a phone; the rest are on the desktop

## 1.8.0
- Added Bat Out!, an arcade baseball game drawn in Chaos Park's cartoon style, seen through the batter's or the pitcher's eyes: swipe through the pitch to hit it, or swipe up from the pitcher to throw (a straight flick for a fastball, a hook for a slider or curveball, a flick back down for a sinker, a pause for a knuckleball)
- Five ways to play from the menu: one turn at bat, one turn on the mound, or a 3, 6 or 9 inning game against the computer with a line score at every change of sides, walk-offs and extra innings
- Every ball in play is fielded by the defence by the rules: fly outs, line outs, ground outs, double plays, sac flies, runners tagging up and taking extra bases, singles to home runs and grand slams
